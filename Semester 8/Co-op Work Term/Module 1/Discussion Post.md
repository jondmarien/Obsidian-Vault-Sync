# Discussion Post - Module 1

---

Hello everyone! I am working as a Full-Stack Security Software Engineer at Hackalyst alongside several of my Sheridan peers for this co-op term. Hackalyst is a web platform that connects hackathons with sponsors and talent, streamlining sponsorships by helping hackathons secure funding faster while giving companies meaningful developer exposure and brand growth opportunities.

The two learning activities I completed from Module 1 are "What is Creative Problem Solving (Foursight Model)" and "How to build a growth mindset." The Foursight Model's four stages - Clarification, Ideation, Development, and Implementation - perfectly mirror how I approach security challenges at Hackalyst. When securing our tech stack, I first clarify what vulnerabilities exist, brainstorm potential solutions and attack vectors, develop and test security measures, then implement comprehensive protection protocols. Building a growth mindset has been essential in cybersecurity since new threats emerge constantly. Staying curious and open to learning helps me adapt to evolving security landscapes and implement cutting-edge protection measures.

Recently, I applied critical thinking and problem-solving skills when transitioning our application from using environment variables for sensitive data to implementing HCP Vault secrets management. Instead of rushing into a quick migration, I used the systematic approach from the Foursight Model. I clarified the security risks of our current setup, researched various secret management solutions, developed a comprehensive migration plan that wouldn't disrupt our services, then implemented HCP Vault with proper access controls and rotation policies. I also secured our Digital Ocean droplet by configuring UFW firewall rules and implementing secure access features. This methodical approach eliminated potential data breaches and significantly strengthened our overall security posture.

One competency I need to develop further is Creativity and Innovation. While I love programming, finding bugs, and thinking through complex issues, I sometimes approach security problems too conventionally. The satisfaction of translating ideas from my head into working code is incredible, but I need to think more creatively about unconventional attack vectors that innovative hackers might exploit. To improve this, I plan to continue to participate in capture-the-flag competitions, both Jeopardy & King of the Hill styles, engage with cybersecurity communities, and practice red-team thinking exercises. I'll also study how experienced security professionals approach novel threats with creative solutions, by constantly staying up-to-date with the latest attacks and breaches.

These reflections have solidified my passion for cybersecurity engineering. The field's constant evolution means I'll always be learning and problem-solving, which aligns perfectly with what I love most about programming. The critical thinking and systematic problem-solving skills I'm developing will be fundamental whether I pursue penetration testing, security architecture, or secure software development. This co-op experience has shown me that protecting digital platforms requires both technical expertise and creative thinking to stay ahead of emerging threats.
